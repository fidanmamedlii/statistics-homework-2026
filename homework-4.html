THE STORY · WHAT ARE n AND m?

A score is just a running tally

Imagine there are n systems to inspect. An attacker visits each system once. The visit adds a random amount to that attacker's penetration score. One attacker makes one length-n path. With m attackers, we simulate m independent paths; they let us see a distribution instead of just one lucky or unlucky outcome.

At step i, the change is Xi. For attacker j, the running score after k systems is Sk(j) = X1(j) + ··· + Xk(j). Each step and each attacker's path uses independent draws from the selected model. These scores are a simplified mathematical model, not a claim that real intrusions are independent or have these exact probabilities.

Two separate knobs: increase n to give every attacker more system visits; increase m to simulate more attackers. Increasing n changes the distribution of each person's score. Increasing m gives a better view of that distribution and makes the whole group's average more stable.

WHAT THE MATH PREDICTS

Three ways to look at the same score

The total grows with the number of steps. The average divides that growing total by n. The normalized total divides by √n, which is the scale of typical random fluctuations around the mean.

ModelIncrementSₙ: mean, varianceSₙ/n: mean, varianceSₙ/√n: mean, varianceLimit idea
Bernoulli1 with probability p; else 0np; np(1−p)p; p(1−p)/np√n; p(1−p)The average converges to p. Uncentered Sₙ/√n does not approach a fixed distribution for p>0; it drifts right. The CLT centers first: (Sₙ−np)/√(np(1−p)) → N(0,1), for 0<p<1.
Rademacher+1 or −1, each with probability 1/20; n0; 1/n0; 1The average converges in probability to 0. By the CLT, Sₙ/√n → N(0,1). At each finite n, only discrete values of the same parity as n occur.
Law of Large Numbers (LLN): if independent steps have the same mean μ and finite variance σ², then Sₙ/n gets close to μ with probability approaching 1. Chebyshev gives P(|Sₙ/n−μ| ≥ ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²), which shrinks as n grows.
Central Limit Theorem (CLT): after subtracting the expected total and dividing by its standard deviation, a sum of many independent, identically distributed steps with finite, nonzero variance becomes approximately standard normal. For the symmetric Rademacher model that is exactly Sₙ/√n. Bernoulli needs centering by np as well as scaling.

“Variance” is a measure of spread, the expected squared distance from the mean. Dividing a variable by n divides its variance by n²; dividing it by √n divides variance by n. A variance of 1 for Sₙ/√n says its typical spread remains visible while its distribution shape approaches a bell curve. The CLT describes the distribution across repeated attackers, not what any one specific attack must do.

READING THE SIMULATED NETWORK

System size controls path length; attacker count controls repetition

ChangePer-attacker effectEffect on the group of m attackers
More systems nMore steps let positive and negative Rademacher increments cancel; Sₙ typically has size √n, but its average shrinks in spread like 1/√n. Bernoulli total grows near np and average settles near p.There are mn visits overall. The pooled average has variance σ²/(mn), so it is more stable.
More attackers mIt does not change the mathematical path of a fixed attacker.Provides more independent observations, so the histogram and empirical moments better describe the model. The group sum's variance is m times the one-attacker variance; the group average's variance falls by a factor m. Rademacher T/√(mn) stays at variance 1.

Model limitations: real attackers do not necessarily act independently, visit every system once, or have equal success probabilities. Defenders change attacker behavior; systems differ; incidents cluster. Bernoulli and Rademacher scores are deliberately simple tools for understanding sums, averages, variance, the LLN, and the CLT—not a real breach forecast.
