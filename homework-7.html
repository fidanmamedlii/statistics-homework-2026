What the test is asking

H₀: X and F are independent in the population. Knowing X does not change the probabilities of F.

H₁: the variables are associated; the distribution of F differs across values of X.

The test compares observed cell counts with expected counts under H₀. A large gap across cells produces a large χ² statistic.

Expected count Eᵢⱼ = (row totalᵢ × column totalⱼ) / N
χ² = Σ (Oᵢⱼ − Eᵢⱼ)² / Eᵢⱼ
degrees of freedom = (rows − 1)(columns − 1)

O means observed. N is the total number of paired observations.

HOW TO READ IT · WITHOUT OVERCLAIMING

From counts to a careful conclusion

1 · Marginal frequencies

Count each category on its own. Row totals describe X; column totals describe F. Divide a total by N to get its marginal proportion.

2 · Conditional frequencies

Hold one category fixed. For P(F | X), divide a cell by its row total. For P(X | F), divide by its column total.

3 · Independence check

Use the margins to calculate expected counts if the variables were independent, then compare those with observed counts through χ².

A small worked example

In the phishing-training demo, 60 trained people include 8 clickers and 52 non-clickers. Overall, 36 of 120 people clicked and 84 did not.

Expected trained-and-clicked = 60 × 36 / 120 = 18
Expected trained-and-not-clicked = 60 × 84 / 120 = 42
Observed trained-and-clicked = 8, so its χ² contribution is (8 − 18)² / 18 ≈ 5.56

Repeat the same calculation for every cell and add the contributions. One cell’s difference is not the whole test.

What a p-value does—and does not—say

A small p-value means the observed table would be unusual if independence and the test assumptions were true. At the chosen α, it can be evidence against H₀.

It is not the probability that H₀ is true, and a large p-value does not prove independence. Report the table, effect size, p-value, and study context.

Cramér’s V helps describe association strength. Statistical significance and practical importance are different questions.

Association is not causation. If two categories are associated, this table alone cannot show that changing X causes F. A third factor could affect both, or the direction could be reversed. Causal claims need an appropriate study design and subject-matter reasoning.
Assumptions and small counts. Each record should be an independent observation, categories should be mutually exclusive, and the table should contain counts. The chi-square p-value is an approximation. This page warns when expected counts are small; for a sparse 2×2 table, consider an exact method such as Fisher’s exact test or collect more data. Do not combine categories unless the combination makes sense before looking at the result.
